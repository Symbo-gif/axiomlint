# AMD-002: Bounded delegated project decisions

## Conflict and purpose

The charter currently designates Michael as approver and prohibits agent
self-approval. The user now wishes the assistant to make decisions on his behalf
while strictly following governance and rigorous science. That instruction
permits ordinary technical judgment now, but reserved formal approvals need
an explicit constitutional rule, not an undocumented interpretation.

This amendment proposes human-authorized delegation for bounded non-release
project decisions while keeping author/reviewer/approver roles distinct.
It does not assert that a human personally reviewed each later artifact.

## Prospective replacement rule

After ratification, interpret charter references to maintainer/owner approval
for ordinary work-package scope, readiness and non-release administrative
closure as permitting either:

1. Michael's actual recorded decision; or
2. a disclosed delegated decision under DEC-004 and this amendment, bound to the
   exact declaration, source revision and evidence digests, with the delegate's
   real identity/context, rationale, date, limits and authority reference.

The implementer cannot approve their own scope extension or certify their own
independent review. A delegated readiness/closure approver must be a genuinely
separate context from the implementation author and may not replace or rewrite
the independent S2/delta-review verdict. If those roles cannot be separated,
the formal decision remains blocked or goes to Michael.

For package closure, every mandatory criterion, distinct S1-S4 state/commit,
clean local gate, independent S2 and S3-delta review, finding disposition and
required risk approval must already exist. Delegated administrative closure
attests that those records meet the preapproved rules; it does not create the
missing evidence, certify scientific truth, or imply personal human review.

## Delegated scientific judgment

Within approved scope, choose appropriate definitions, conservative semantics,
resource budgets and analysis methods before affected data exists. Record
assumptions and alternatives. Quantitative thresholds require justified
preregistration, not convenient default numbers.

EXP-E2 stays an independent pre-execution review. The protocol author cannot
supply that review or their own approval. Null, negative, unexpected, failed
and inconclusive outcomes remain retained. An error affecting new measurements
needs a newly authorized correction cycle; no favorable retries or silent
metric, seed, denominator, exclusion or stopping-rule changes.

## Reserved decisions and unchanged prohibitions

Michael retains constitutional amendments/delegation expansion, material
scope expansion, material residual-risk acceptance and release authorization.
EV-E6 still needs its separately required independent correspondence review
and two authorized approvals; ordinary delegation does not replace them.

No D1/D2 carry, fabricated signature/evidence, self-audit, missing-session
closure, unapproved hostile execution or mandatory proprietary dependency.
Local-only tests and no hosted CI remain mandatory.
Public pushing/publication, payment/purchase, host installation, external source
disclosure and signing credentials require separate exact-scope authorization.
The current grant alone does not authorize them.

## Baseline, effect and revocation

Existing evidence and session states remain historical. No delegated approval
is backdated; no pending charter or package becomes accepted merely because
the assistant drafted this amendment. If ratified, the ratification identifies
the exact amendment digest and effective source revision.

The existing 144 briefs remain the versioned planning baseline. This amendment,
once ratified, takes precedence over lower-level human-only wording only for
the explicitly delegated decisions above; all other entry/exit controls remain.
Approved session records must name the applicable brief and authority versions.

Michael may revoke or narrow the grant. On revocation or an authority conflict,
stop the affected formal decisions, preserve records and seek resolution;
do not delete or impersonate the former delegate.
